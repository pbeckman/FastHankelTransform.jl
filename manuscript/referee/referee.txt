Reviewer #1: Review for ``A Butterfly-Accelerated Manifold Harmonic Transform''
by Beckman, Potter, and O'Neil.

This paper considers the problem of rapidly computing the discrete manifold
harmonic transform (MHT) $$ f(\boldsymbol{x}_j) = \sum_{k=1}^m c_k
\varphi_k(\boldsymbol{x}_j), \quad \text{for } j = 1,\ldots,n, $$ where
$\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_m$ are the eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami
operator for some choice of boundary conditions (corresponding to eigenvalues $0
\le \lambda_1 \le \cdots \le \lambda_m$), $c_1,\ldots,c_m$ are coefficients, and
$\boldsymbol{x}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{x}_n$ are points on the manifold. The paper
introduces a fast algorithm for computing the MHT called the
Butterfly-Accelerated Manifold Harmonic Transform (BF-MHT) and provides some
theoretical and empirical evidence that the method is effective.

This paper contributes to the literature on fast harmonic transforms by
presenting a novel computational and theoretical framework for developing and
analyzing general fast manifold harmonic transforms. The paper introduces a
butterfly algorithm based on Fiedler trees that is widely applicable. The paper
provides some theoretical results about this algorithm in the special case in
which the manifold is the flat torus $[-\pi,\pi]^2$, and provides empirical
evidence of the general effectiveness of the algorithm. By taking an approach
based on Fiedler trees, the paper is able to treat questions of how to
discretize a manifold and compute the eigenfunctions as independent questions,
enabling progress on the question of fast algorithm development. There are
several technical issues and missing details that should be addressed, but the
key ideas in the theoretical and computational results are sound.

I recommend a minor revision.

I have listed two main comments and several minor comments below.


Main Comments:

1) The presentation and statement of Lemma 7 require some clarification. This
result provides a connection between the analytic and numerical results, and
raises questions for further investigation. The following points could use
clarification:

1a) In Equation (5.13) the authors provide a heuristic argument based on the
Jacobi-Anger expansion that explains why ``one cannot generally hope to obtain
an expansion of $K$ with $\varepsilon$-rank less than $bR$ which is valid for
$\boldsymbol{\omega}$ in any domain containing the circle of radius $b$.''
However, in the paragraph before Lemma 7 it is stated that in the pre-asymptotic
regime ``we observe empirically that the $\varepsilon$-rank of the
annulus-to-disk Fourier kernel $K : A_a^b \to B_R$ grows roughly like
$(bR)^{2/3}$ rather than like $b R$''. It would be helpful to directly address
these seemingly conflicting statements. Replacing the heuristic argument with a
precise lower bound could help clarify this situation.

1b) The statement of Lemma 7 says ``Choose $C_1$ and $C_2$ such that...'' which
seems to imply that these constants are chosen together and may depend on one
another. However, if we are allowed to choose $C_2$ in terms of $C_1$, then the
condition in Equation (5.34) does not seem to strengthen the condition in
Equation (5.23) in Theorem 5. I may be misunderstanding something so
clarification would be helpful. In particular, clarify all constant dependencies
and explain what makes the result an improvement instead of a finite range
special case.

2) Many of the proofs in the paper are incomplete or lack details. In each case,
the critical scaling is derived, so I think the results are all plausibly
correct, up to constants, but many results are not established as written. For
example, in some of the numerical rank bounds the coefficients in an infinite
series are estimated, but the tail of the series is not formally bounded. I have
detailed many instances of technical gaps in the proofs in the minor comments
below, but the authors should also carefully check all proofs for completeness.


Minor Comments:

[Page 1 Eq. (1.1)] The notation for the index set of the sum is informal. Write
$\sum_{\boldsymbol{k} \in K}$ and then define the index set $K$ precisely (the
authors could consider making a simplifying assumption such as $m = 4 q^2$ if
desired).

[Page 2 line 1] Say ``Riemannian $d$-manifold'' if a Riemannian structure is
assumed.

[Page 3 line 7] You briefly mention the fast adjoint transform which maps
function values to expansion coefficients. Does the approach presented in the
paper also immediately provide a fast algorithm for the adjoint of the MHT? If
so, consider adding a remark about this. Even though this is an adjoint and not
an inverse, it could still be useful for estimating coefficients in combination
with least squares or appropriate quadrature weights.

[Pages 3--8] The background material on the butterfly factorization, Fiedler
trees, and streaming butterfly factorization is well written, but long. The
authors could consider moving some of this material to later in the paper.

[Page 9 Lemma 1] The phrase ``For any $\xi > 0$.'' seems misplaced, e.g., write
``Then, for any $\xi > 0$, we have''


[Page 9 Theorem 2] Writing $e^{i \rho r \cos(\alpha-\theta)} = e^{i \rho r
\cos(\alpha) \cos(\theta)} e^{i \rho r \sin(\alpha) \sin(\theta)}$ and expanding
the exponentials in Taylor series and using tail bounds on the series is an easy
way to see that $r_\varepsilon = \mathcal{O}((b R +
\log(\varepsilon^{-1}))^2)$. It might be worth mentioning this to motivate the
result since the proof is technical and involves identities with which the
reader may be less familiar.

[Page 10 line 10] The proof ends by counting integer pairs $(k,\ell)$
corresponding to terms satisfying a bound. It remains show that the sum of the
omitted terms is less than $\varepsilon$ to complete the proof.


[Page 10 after Eq. (5.13)] The statement ``As $|J_k(b R)|$ is $\mathcal{O}(1)$
for $k < b R$'' just says that $|J_k(b R)|$ is upper bounded and does not
justify a lower bound on the $\varepsilon$-rank. Clarify this argument.

[Page 11 line 18] It would be helpful to write out the equation for $c_{k
\ell}(r)$ at the start of the proof for the convenience of the reader.

[Page 11 last line] Why is neglecting the second term in the numerator
justified? This term is positive and seems like it could be large. Also, write
out the details for truncating the sum over $\ell$ to complete this
proof. (Currently the proof seems to end before the result is established. The
tail of the series should be bounded).

[Page 12 Eq. (5.25)] Why do you use the expansion from Lemma 3 here? It seems
more natural to first truncate the sum using Lemma 1. I do not follow the
argument as written.

[Page 12 line 16] ``counting the number of pairs $(k,\ell)$ for which
$|c_{k,\ell}(r)|$ may exceed $\varepsilon$ gives the result''. This does not
seem to establish the result as it remains to bound the sum of the omitted
terms.

[Page 13 line 5 from the bottom] Writing $\ell = \mathcal{O}(L/3)$ is imprecise,
since the big-$\mathcal{O}$ has an implied constant. You could say the estimates
balance at $\ell = L/3$ or find another notation.

[Page 14 Section 6] The authors should consider making their code publicly
available, e.g., by posting the code on GitHub, to improve the reproducibility
of the numerical experiments.

[Page 14--15] When referencing Figure 7, it would help to specify which plot you
are referring to, e.g., in Figure 7 (left)...

[Page 15 Figure 7 (right)] The figure legend references Thm. 5.5, but the
theorem numberings in the paper do not include the section numbers.






Reviewer #2: The paper presents a nice algorithm for fast matvec products
between eigenfunctions of Laplacian eigenfunctions on manifolds and coefficient
vectors. To the best of my knowledge, it is novel and the numerical experiments
suggest it is valuable. However, the theoretical results currently do not
motivate the algorithm's efficiency in the general case. The theoretical results
focus on the case of the torus, in which case the matvec products become just a
DFT. This is understandable, because as the authors mention, deriving rank
bounds in the case of general manifolds is difficult. The consequence of the
absence of rank bounds is however that the algorithm is more empirically
motivated than theoretically grounded, and it is my opinion that some stronger
theoretical results would be needed to be a good fit for ACHA.

The numerical experiments show the usefulness of the proposed algorithm for a
number of different applications. These are valuable and interesting to see, but
some more details of the experiments could be added to strengthen the
experimental side, which would be especially important in light of the comments
about the theory above. The authors mention that storage cost is a proxy for the
computational cost of applying the matvec products and therefore do not show any
timings. This is true, but it would nonetheless be valuable to a reader to see
the actual timings (compared to dense matvec products) to know how practical the
algorithms currently are, and if there is some room for improvement. In the case
of the torus, mentioning the timing of a DFT on the used hardware would also
give a good sense of the relative speeds. Moreover, showing the timing of the
precomputation step (including precomputing the eigenfunctions, and the quad
tree using the Fiedler tree construction) would be valuable. It would then be
valuable to know which hardware the algorithm was run on.
